October 2026 Windows development preview

Run a pixel-art café for visiting witches. The October Unity 6 development build adds a warm oak-wood room, an interactive café journal, and the first chapter, Lantern Tea Party.

  • Accept delivery orders, cook and serve dishes, research recipes, and install permanent shop improvements.
  • Complete three witches’ commissions across multiple days, collect their letters, and prepare the chapter’s tea-party finale.
  • Save during a working day and resume your café, orders, and progress. Switch between English and Chinese in the title screen or settings.

Availability: The October Windows update is being prepared and verified; a new itch.io download is not available yet. The older demo remains available there. Features and screenshots in this development preview show work in progress.

1. Overview

A cozy café game combining time management, dual-character perspective, and resource strategy.

Tulips Café is a simulation game that merges elements of time management, resource management, and dual perspective game play.

The game features two primary playable characters: Anne, the café owner who handles resources and strategy,

Jessica, the waitress who manages orders, customer interactions, and dish preparations. The player switches between both characters to optimize the café’s operations while interacting with a diverse set of customer witches.

Anne and Jessica

2. Gameplay & Mechanics

tips

Core Gameplay Loop:

Switching Perspectives:

The player can switch between Anne and Jessica. Each character has distinct responsibilities and tasks:

Anne: Manages resources, unlock new dishes and researches recipes. Jessica: Fulfill orders, serves dishes and interacts with customers The switch consumes Action Points(AP),which are limited each day. This mechanic forces players to prioritize tasks and make strategic decisions on when to switch between the characters.

Resource Management:

Essence: A magical resource used to unlock new recipes and upgrade the café’s offerings.

Gold: A more mundane resource used for daily purchases like Essence and upgrades.

Reputation: Gained by serving all the delivery orders and witches within today.

Resources

Orders and Customer Interaction:

Witches: Order their preference dishes and generous on tips. It is easily predicted that serving witches are far better than completing golem delivery orders, however, golem delivery orders are far more stable than witch orders because player don’t get to get control of the timing of witches’ arrival. If your Reputation is high, the witches are more likely to visit your café, which means you are more likely to get more money. Be aware that the frequent visit of witches may cause you don’t have enough AP to complete all the orders, which means your reputation will drop.

7. AI Balancing & Playtesting

In the early development stages, a Python-based version of this game was built to emulate the core gameplay loops and optimize the game’s parameters. This simulation ran thousands of test scenarios to determine ideal AP cost structures, resource balance, and customer interaction probabilities. AI Playtesting: The AI was used to “autoplay” the game, making decisions about which recipes to unlock, when to switch characters, and how to allocate resources. By running these simulations, the development team gathered valuable insights on game balance. The use of AI allowed us to make data-driven decisions about the game’s economy and mechanics, ensuring that the progression curve is challenging but fair for players. The AI simulations provided a foundation for future updates and expansions, allowing the design to evolve and scale. With the help of this magical numerical designer, Players are more likely to get into the Flow while playing our game

8. Target Players

16–35Simulation, cozy storytellingStardew Valley, Recettear

Player Demographics: Age Range: 16–35 years old Interests: Players interested in casual simulation games, resource management, and narrative-driven experiences. Gaming Preferences: Ideal for players who enjoy games like Stardew Valley, Recettear, and Overcooked. The gameplay is easy to pick up, but there’s enough depth for players to optimize and strategize their operations. Game Accessibility: The game is designed to be accessible to both casual players and those seeking a deeper, more strategic experience. The dual-character system offers variety, and the AP limit system creates pacing that encourages decision-making/management.
